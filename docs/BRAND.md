# Brand brief — Earn Your Skyline

**Status: DRAFT, 26 Nov 2026.** Written by Claude from the game as it stands:
its taglines, its art and its rules. Anything marked **[owner?]** is a guess
the owner needs to confirm or replace. Once agreed, this page is the yardstick
for the logo, the design system and every screen. If a design choice can't be
defended from this page, it's the wrong choice.

---

## 1. What the game is, in one sentence

A career sim for your phone where you start with almost nothing (a debt, a
borrowed desk, one rented kitchen) and build a life one week at a time. Each
profession breaks in its own way.

## 2. What "earning your skyline" means

The skyline is not a city you conquer. It is **your own place in it**: one lit
window in a tall building, late at night, that is yours because you paid for
every floor beneath it. You earn it slowly, honestly and expensively. Nothing
is handed to you, and nothing is permanent unless you keep working.

## 3. Who it's for **[owner?]**

- **Adults, roughly 20 to 40,** who like a slow, thoughtful game on their
  phone: a few minutes a day, one decision at a time.
- They enjoy management sims, but find most of them shallow ("tap to collect
  coins") or childish.
- Some are in, or dream of, the careers the game is about. They like that it
  respects how those careers actually work.
- **Not** aimed at children or at players looking for fast action.

## 4. How it should feel: three words **[owner?]**

1. **Earned.** Every win has a cost on the screen next to it. No confetti,
   no "AMAZING!", no rewards for nothing.
2. **Late-night.** Quiet, warm lamplight in a dark city. The hour when you
   are still working because it matters to you. The art already lives here.
3. **Honest.** Plain words, real numbers and dry humour. The game tells you
   exactly what something will cost and lets you decide. (The taglines are the
   model: "a supplier who wants cash up front, and produce that is worth less
   every hour.")

## 5. What it must NOT look or feel like

- **A finance dashboard or a hospital system.** No "clinical" teal
  everywhere, no walls of monospace numbers, no spreadsheet grids. The
  interface grew out of the Physician's medical screens, and it still shows.
- **A casino or a cash-grab mobile game.** No gold coins, sparkles, loot
  boxes, flashing "SALE" banners, or arrows shooting upward.
- **Stock "success" imagery:** stairs, rising arrows, rockets, trophies,
  people in suits high-fiving. The current logo uses three of these.
- **Esports or sticker style:** heavy outlines, slanted letters, neon.
- **Childish:** cartoon mascots, bouncy fonts, primary colours.

## 6. What it should look like (to be tested in the design work)

- **Colour.**
  - The ground is deep navy-black, as now.
  - The primary colour is **warm lamplight** (amber), not medical teal.
  - Each career gets one quiet accent of its own, for example a copper for
    the Chef or an ink-blue for the Solicitor, so you always know whose life
    you are in.
  - Red and green are kept only for money going wrong or right.
- **Type.** Two typefaces at most:
  - a characterful display face for names and headings (editorial, slightly
    literary; candidates to test: a modern serif, or a refined condensed sans)
  - a calm, highly readable sans for everything else

  Numbers in tabular figures, but not in a "computer terminal" mono everywhere.
- **Imagery.** The cinematic night photography we already have (see
  `VISUALS.md`). It is the strongest part of the game's identity; the
  interface should step back and let it lead.
- **Motion.** Slow and settled: things fade and rise, never bounce.
  `prefers-reduced-motion` is always respected.
- **Shape.** Calm cards, generous spacing, and one clear action per screen.
  On a phone the thumb should never have to hunt.

## 7. The logo: what it has to do

- **Read at 16px** (a browser tab) and **at 180px** (an iPhone home-screen
  icon), on dark and on light.
- **Be a real vector (SVG) drawing,** not an image-model raster. Image models
  can explore ideas; the final mark is redrawn clean.
- **Carry the idea in section 2,** not a generic "growth" symbol.
- **Work without the words** (as the app icon) and **with them** (as the
  title screen lockup).

Three directions to explore, in order of preference **[owner?]**:
1. **The lit window.** A slim tower silhouette at night with a single warm,
   lit window. It says "your place in the city", matches the lamp-lit art, and
   is simple enough for an icon.
2. **The wordmark.** "Skyline" (or "Earn Your Skyline") set in a carefully
   chosen display face, with one small architectural detail, such as a
   stepped setback on one letter. No picture.
3. **The setback S.** A capital S drawn as a building's stepped profile, the
   way old skyscrapers narrow as they rise.

## 8. Three references **[owner?]**

The owner should name three apps, games or brands whose look feels right.
Claude's suggestions to react to, not to copy:
- **Monument Valley:** calm, architectural, every screen composed.
- **Reigns:** a narrow, decision-a-moment phone game with a strong single
  style.
- **Kinfolk / The Gentlewoman** (print magazines): editorial type, warm
  photography, lots of space.

## 9. Open questions for the owner

1. Is section 3 (the audience) right?
2. Are "earned, late-night, honest" the right three words? Replace any that
   aren't.
3. Should the name on the icon be "Skyline" or "Earn Your Skyline"?
4. Which logo direction in section 7 should we explore first?
5. Which three apps, games or brands do you want it to feel like?
6. Anything you already love or hate in the current game's look?
